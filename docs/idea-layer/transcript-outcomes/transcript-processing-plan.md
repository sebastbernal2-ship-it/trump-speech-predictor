# Transcript-processing plan

This is a proposed data-readiness workflow, not an implemented pipeline or a claim that the corpus is ready for training. Its purpose is to produce transcript-derived targets for characters, words, sentences, topics, and ideas. Prediction inputs and new models are outside this initial processing task; the existing seven-day benchmark and other research remain separate and unchanged. All target-event transcript products are outcomes, never same-event prediction features.

## Available inputs and scope to resolve

The inventory reports 5,483 `rollcall-events.jsonl` records, including 1,990 speech candidates, 1,554 Q&A candidates, 1,419 other Trump events, and 520 non-Trump events in `rollcall-by-type/`. These are classification views, not independently verified event types. The 1,200-record `trump-long-speeches.jsonl` is another view with target-speaker text. Separately, `events.jsonl` has 1,940 records with turns, `speeches.jsonl` has 1,019 text records with provisional review fields, and `segments.jsonl` has 101,650 segments with speaker and classification fields. Counts are from the supplied inventory, not a newly verified census. These files may overlap; do not add their counts as unique speeches. Event records sampled locally contain IDs, date, source and reuse metadata, ordered speaker turns, raw and processed text, and timestamp information; the long-speech view has text and aggregate counts. Segment classifications and provisional reviews are not ground truth.

Required inputs are the chosen immutable source records and their version/hash; event and source identifiers, date and source URL where available; ordered turns and speaker identity; transcript text; and available provenance, timestamps, and reuse terms. Missing or uncertain fields stay explicitly unknown. The owner must decide the population meant by “every speech,” including Q&A, mixed events, duplicate versions and non-Trump speakers. Until then create an inventory and eligibility/review status for every source record, not a silently filtered corpus. Check source rights before any new distribution or provider submission.

## Proposed processing and artifacts

1. **Inventory and reconcile.** Record source file/version, record ID, source/event metadata, text availability and candidate type. Identify possible duplicate or overlapping records and map them to canonical event and transcript-version IDs with evidence and unresolved matches. Retain all source records and distinguish alternate versions from actual duplicates; do not merge on date/title alone. Report counts at each step and excluded or unresolved records with reasons.
2. **Select speaker and preserve evidence.** For the agreed population, order turns and identify target-speaker turns using source speaker IDs, labels and aliases. Flag ambiguous attribution, missing text, anomalies and incomplete transcripts for review. Retain original turns and their raw text, source references and order. Build a versioned working transcript with explicit separators and a mapping back to turn IDs and raw offsets; do not accidentally include another speaker. A flattened speech view without turn provenance needs reconciliation or an explicit limitation.
3. **Clean with an audit trail.** Define and version rules for whitespace, markup, annotations, encoding and transcription artifacts; preserve original wording, punctuation and disfluencies unless a documented rule changes them. Produce a clean-text version, transformation log and raw-to-clean span mapping, including unmappable spans and review flags. Never overwrite source data. The extraction evidence must resolve back to preserved source text.
4. **Mechanically separate surface layers.** From the same frozen clean text, produce ordered character units, exact word units and period-delimited sentence segments, with event/transcript version, sequence, offsets, original surface text, rule version and status. Preserve whitespace, periods and all other separators as spans or an equivalent lossless representation so the clean text can be reconstructed. Period splitting is the requested mechanical rule, not a claim of linguistic sentence boundaries: abbreviations, decimals, ellipses, repeated periods and trailing text need documented behavior and examples. Define character unit and word boundaries before implementation; do not silently normalize case, spelling or punctuation. Validate reconstruction, ordering and offset references.
5. **Extract semantic layers.** Extract candidate transcript mentions with exact source-linked evidence; group or abstract them into specific ideas and broader topics, preserving many-to-many links and uncertainty rather than forcing a hierarchy. Record extractor/version, prompts or configuration, input hash, citations, review status and failures. Existing layered-outcome and topic prototypes are starting points for investigation, not validated gold or a complete corpus. A structural or exact-quote check does not establish semantic support or completeness.
6. **Review and release.** Validate schemas, identity, attribution, offsets, reconstruction, citations, and coverage of the agreed population. Use independent human references for semantic support, omissions, topic/idea boundaries and inter-annotator disagreement; adjudicate or retain unresolved cases. Publish a versioned manifest and per-event layer artifacts with provenance, processing versions, eligibility, exclusions, errors and review/quality status. Distinguish a reviewed empty result from missing input, extraction failure and not yet processed. A release is ready for supervised work only when the agreed population and quality criteria are met for every layer; partial outputs remain explicitly partial.

Final outputs are linked, ordered character, word and period-split sentence targets plus evidence-backed idea and topic inventories per eligible transcript. Intermediate artifacts include the inventory and identity crosswalk, preserved source turns, cleaned text and mappings, extraction candidates, review decisions and validation reports. IDs and versioned mappings should allow each target to be traced back to its input without duplicating or overwriting the source. These outputs do not define future feature sets, training splits or prediction architectures.

## Tentative semantics and open questions

A **topic** is tentatively a short, broad concept naming what was discussed, such as “war.” An **idea** is a more particular unit, such as the Ukrainian war; an idea may eventually include the direction and degree of an expressed opinion. These examples are provisional rather than a finished ontology. The existing research uses more specific talking-point definitions; reconcile them through a versioned annotation experiment rather than silently relabeling old artifacts.

- Which events and speakers constitute every speech, and how are mixed speeches, interviews, incomplete transcripts, duplicates and alternate sources handled?
- Which source text is authoritative when raw and processed turns differ, and which cleaning transformations are acceptable without changing meaning or exact words?
- What counts as a character (code point or grapheme), a word, and a separator? How should contractions, hyphens, numbers and punctuation be represented? What exactly happens at each period and at empty or trailing segments?
- What granularity distinguishes an idea from a topic or a claim, and when should repeated, related, implied or contradictory passages merge or split? Can an idea belong to multiple topics?
- Should stance and its degree be labels now, and what evidence and uncertainty scale would support them? Vocal tonality needs separately available, aligned audio and is future exploration, not an inference from transcript text alone.
- What independent annotation sample, adjudication process and layer-specific quality thresholds make the outputs usable, and how are long transcripts and failures reviewed?
- How should available news and social sources be inventoried in parallel, and where should the historical event-to-idea study fit? Source selection, temporal relevance and future model inputs remain undecided.

## Cleanup needed before implementation

Reconcile the overlapping data views and speaker/turn identities; document source provenance, reuse terms and missingness; inspect and version existing cleaning and labeling rules; separate provisional classifications from reviewed labels; map existing semantic artifacts to their source transcript versions; and record which records/layers have actually been processed and reviewed. Define a reproducible artifact layout and validation/reporting contract without moving, deleting or overwriting local data. Review existing code and tests for reuse and gaps before changing them. This work does not establish that dashboard, market, baseline, graph or other repository components are obsolete.
