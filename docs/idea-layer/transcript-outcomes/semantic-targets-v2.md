# Review-driven semantic targets v2

`semantic-targets-v2` is a separate development approach, not a replacement for the frozen benchmark or a validated automated extractor. `speechprefix.transcript_processing.semantic_v2.build(record, review)` takes a source record with ordered `{turn_id, text}` turns and a review bound to the SHA-256 of its canonical JSON (sorted keys, ASCII escaping, compact separators). Preserve that exact source record, its immutable file/version locator and review alongside the result: the hash identifies content, not its storage location. The output records the version, hash, reviewer, explicit status, segment decisions, clean mappings and targets. The review is an input produced by a person or another semantic extractor; this code does not infer meanings or establish their truth, completeness or quality. Record the reviewer or extractor identity, instructions/configuration and date in the retained review. The output is a transcript outcome, never a same-event prediction feature.

## Definitions and procedure

A **topic** is a short broad concept actually discussed, such as employment. An **idea** is a more specific proposition or subject, such as a factory creating jobs. Each target is separately enumerated and has an ID, label and one or more direct target-speaker citations; several ideas can share a topic, and neither forced hierarchy nor stance is inferred. Capture opinion only when explicitly supported by the words; direction, degree and vocal tone are not automatically classified. Avoid converting a question into a claim by the target. A target may cite a preceding question as *context*, but must also cite the answer as speaker evidence, and an ambiguous answer should remain uncertain. Context is not itself a target-speaker statement.

Review all turns and partition each original turn into contiguous half-open code-point spans. Label each span `target`, `context`, `metadata`, or `uncertain`, with a reason. This explicitly excludes headings, dates, subjects, annotations and other speakers from direct evidence while preserving them in the source. Resolve attribution and mixed turns from evidence, not names alone; unknown speaker, incomplete transcript or questionable boundaries require uncertainty. Retain questions as context, including those outside the selected answer. This is an explicit span review, not a general automatic metadata detector; errors in the review remain possible.

The cleaner collapses whitespace within each reviewed segment and trims its edges, retaining a raw turn offset span for each clean character. No words, punctuation or disfluencies are silently rewritten. Each citation identifies a turn and exact raw offsets and is reconstructed from that source. Citations cannot cross segment boundaries or use metadata as evidence. Repeated identical phrases remain distinguishable by offsets. A citation proves location only, not semantic entailment; manual review must check support, granularity, attribution and omissions. Keep separate semantic units when warranted and avoid claiming completeness based on lexical coverage. Longer inputs should be reviewed in ordered chunks with overlapping context and an explicit cross-chunk consolidation review; this implementation does not automate chunking or guarantee coverage.

`unprocessed` means no extraction was attempted; `failed` requires a reason and contains no results; `uncertain` requires a reason and may contain partial candidates; `reviewed_empty` is an explicit reviewer decision after examining nonempty target speech; `reviewed` contains at least one target. Neither an empty lexical match nor no target speaker permits `reviewed_empty`. Incomplete input, unknown attribution or an unresolved segment cannot be represented as a completed reviewed result. A reviewer must choose `uncertain` for any unresolved semantic or source question; this is a policy requirement, not something code can detect. Separate no-target disposition and source eligibility remain open. No state implies independent adjudication.

## Development demonstrations

Run `PYTHONPATH=. python3 tests/transcript_processing/test_semantic_v2.py`. The unit examples are deliberately constructed paraphrases of the documented diagnostic cases, not independent annotations. Additionally, `PYTHONPATH=. python3 scripts/semantic_v2_diagnostic.py /path/to/speeches.jsonl` reads the actual documented lines 222, 291, 968 and 969, checks source-bound offsets and prints row hashes and provisional targets without copying source text. Those hand-selected examples remain uncertain because attribution and inventory completeness are not verified. They illustrate the intended review: health/well-wishing and the job-number claim can be entered despite lexical omissions; mourning Iran attack victims and investment policy are specific ideas; a United Nations statement does not turn “right now” into a rights topic; and an American Pope statement is represented separately. A title and a reporter's question cannot be cited as target evidence. The Syria question and short affirmative answer demonstrate context linked separately from the answer. A repeated long example exercises raw offsets; a reviewed empty inventory and unprocessed, failed and uncertain states exercise distinct outcomes. These examples are hand-authored inputs to the interface, not evidence that the system automatically finds the reported cases, measures accuracy, or generalizes to long speeches. They do not resolve the other reported cases or source attribution across the corpus.

Before releasing training targets, run this procedure on immutable actual source records with separately retained reviews, establish independent blinded references and adjudication, predeclare semantic matching and quality thresholds, and measure omissions and unsupported content across a representative sample. Speaker selection, source completeness, target eligibility and all corpus coverage remain unresolved.
