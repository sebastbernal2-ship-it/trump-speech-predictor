Status: local reconciliation, 2026-11-17. Phase 1 is **not demonstrated ready**. The per-record local crosswalk at `../../../.local-reconciliation/transcript-crosswalk.csv` lists every record in the four inventoried transcript-bearing views, with source line, ID, record and text hashes where applicable, class and segment links, each layer's observed status, and links to the assessed outputs. ## Accounting and method

The ledger covers **9,642 source-view records**, not 9,642 distinct events: 5,483 `rollcall-events.jsonl`, 1,940 `events.jsonl`, 1,019 `speeches.jsonl`, and 1,200 `trump-long-speeches.jsonl`. It has 6,509 distinct ID strings; that is not a verified count of unique speeches. All 5,483 Roll Call records have a class-view ID link; all 101,650 segments link by speech ID to the 1,019-record view. The 1,200 long-speech rows share IDs with Roll Call records; 1,933 of 1,940 event-view rows do so, leaving seven with no ID link. The record hashes differ for these joins, which are links between views, not proof of identical text. Different IDs, including government and Roll Call sources, are not merged based on title or date. Eligibility, alternate versions, attribution, and completeness remain unresolved for each row unless separately reviewed.

The candidate join checks source line, ID and raw-text SHA-256: all 1,019 match. Seven have zero topic candidates and 187 have zero idea candidates; the ledger identifies each by its zero count. These are unreviewed lexical no-matches, not negative labels. The 49 final v2.3 and one chunked layered idea rows each match the canonical SHA-256 of a Roll Call source record. Ten additional rows in other views have the same IDs as these outcomes but different record hashes: their links are explicitly marked ID-only, not proof of applicability to that version. The final artifacts are structural candidates, not validated topics or semantic gold.

The ledger's `no_saved_output_found` and `no_assessed_output_found` mean none was located **within this reconciliation's assessed artifacts**, not proof that no output exists anywhere. It assesses the candidate cache and two final layered artifacts named above, not every legacy research file, retry, or source archive. Thus other local subsets and source versions remain to be inventoried before claiming every output or failure has been found. No saved corpus-wide mechanical release was located; all rows show this gap. Every semantic layer remains unreviewed or lacks an assessed output, and no row is established ready for all five layers. Source files had no empty top-level text or empty turn list under the checks performed; this does not establish transcript completeness or speaker correctness.

The earlier v2.2 extraction had nine failures, subsequently retried into the final 49 rows. Four still failed in the DeepSeek retry (`dt45-19-d2856092`, `dt45-19-84ea6909`, `dt45-19-04f3154c`, `dt45-19-60878c24`) and were recovered in the final artifact; they remain semantically unvalidated. The long event `dt45-19-33210f4d` failed single-pass attempts and has one structurally valid chunked candidate, with unresolved cross-chunk and completeness quality. The individual records and artifact locations are in the ledger. Other retry histories require a broader artifact audit.

## Known failures and quality issues

- In the 1,019-row review, examples include a health statement with no idea match; a job-number claim missed; title or subject metadata matched as speech; a reporter's question and another speaker's words attributed as topics; and `right now` matching `right`. Headings, dates, annotations and other speakers survive whitespace cleaning. Evidence integrity does not establish attribution or semantic support. See the six detailed cases and source lines in [readiness review](extraction-readiness-review-v1.md).
- The 49-event layered v2.2 run had nine format/structure failures; retries yielded 49 structurally valid v2.3 rows, with mixed model provenance. A long transcript repeatedly failed single-pass validation; one structurally valid chunked result does not establish reliable coverage or cross-chunk identity. Neither success proves semantic correctness or recall. See [development results](layered-transcript-outcomes-v2-development.md).
- Topic grouping is a one-speech prototype with structural membership checks; its input has an identified possible under-split. Point extraction has both over- and under-splitting. Independent human reference annotation and adjudication have not established semantic accuracy. Prior relation quality bars failed on separate diagnostics; fine relations are not prerequisite to claiming the five basic layers unless adopted in the target contract.
- Missing or partial source text, uncertain speakers, failed extraction, unprocessed records and reviewed empty outcomes must remain distinct. A lexical empty or failed response is never proof of no ideas. Existing counts do not establish complete transcripts, source rights, or the number of distinct events.

## Completion work and evidence by layer

First decide the eligible population, source version and speaker/text policy, including statements, exchanges, metadata and incomplete recordings. Build an immutable source-record ledger keyed by file hash and line/record ID, with evidence-backed canonical event/version links, unresolved duplicate candidates, eligibility and exclusions. Join every output by source and text hashes; record per-layer `not processed`, `failed`, `needs review`, `validated`, or explicitly reviewed empty, with error details and artifact versions. Reconcile all listed views and any additional raw sources discovered, report denominator and unresolved rows, and do not merge records by date/title alone. Preserve originals, rights information, turn order and raw-to-clean mappings. Extend the local crosswalk to all legacy outputs, exact transcript-version matches, eligibility decisions, and review results; the present ledger identifies gaps only against the artifacts it assesses.

| Layer | Changes needed | Evidence needed before readiness |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Characters | Apply the existing code-point separator to the agreed, versioned speaker text; decide whether code points meet the target definition. | Per-record coverage and reconstruction, offsets, source hash and documented handling of Unicode, missing and partial input. |
| Words | Confirm whether whitespace-delimited exact tokens with punctuation attached satisfy the intended word target; version any changed rule. | Per-record exact surfaces, ordering, offsets and separator reconstruction, with edge cases and attribution checks. |
| Sentences | Apply the requested literal-period split on the selected text, retaining empty and trailing segments; do not call the result linguistic sentence segmentation. | Per-record reconstruction and offsets; explicit checks for abbreviations, decimals, ellipses and trailing text. |
| Topics | Define broad concepts and membership, clean speaker-specific evidence, and replace or review the lexical candidates using a consistent versioned method. | Independent references across varied lengths, formats, no-match and multi-speaker cases; adjudicated coverage, precision, grouping coherence and agreed thresholds, plus per-record status. |
| Ideas | Define specific units, repetition, split/merge, uncertainty and whether stance/degree is in scope; test robust long-transcript processing and source-linked evidence. | Independent, blinded reference inventories and adjudication; measure supportedness, omissions, granularity and agreement against frozen criteria, with per-record failure and incomplete-source accounting. |

For all five, publish a local versioned manifest with source and output hashes, rule versions, counts, validation results, explicit unresolved cases and review provenance. Establish and meet quality/coverage thresholds before calling the outputs supervised-learning ready. Structural validation and same-provider model reviews are useful diagnostics, not semantic gold. Target transcripts and derived labels remain outcomes only for their own event; existing benchmark and evaluation remain unchanged.

## Combined processing path and remaining blockers

`python3 -m speechprefix.transcript_processing.layers INPUT.jsonl NEW_OUTPUT_DIR --id-field speech_id --text-field text` now writes one source-linked row per selected top-level text record containing all three mechanical layers and both lexical candidate layers on the same whitespace-cleaned text. The manifest hashes the input file and output, records rule and lexicon versions, and marks the results unreviewed. The output directory must be new and its parent must exist; a failed run leaves `INCOMPLETE` and no valid manifest. See [candidate rules](transcript-candidates.md) and [mechanical rules](mechanical-separation.md); mechanical offsets in this combined path refer to `clean_text`, not the original raw field. `clean_to_raw` links it back to the raw text. Empty candidate arrays do not mean reviewed empty outcomes. Output can be large, especially the per-code-point character arrays; plan disk capacity before a corpus run.

This resolves the missing unified production path, not the corpus readiness gaps. The 1,019 speech rows and 1,200 long-speech rows have top-level text fields suitable for this interface, subject to their separate attribution and eligibility review. The event views use turns and require an explicit, evidence-backed speaker selection and mapping before use; flattening all speakers would repeat the observed attribution defect. The existing lexical matcher still includes headings and other speakers if supplied, can match `right` in `right now`, and misses ideas such as those in the readiness review. Resolving those semantic defects requires an agreed speaker/text policy and independent annotated references, not an unvalidated rule or an empty candidate list treated as a negative label. Long-transcript semantic coverage, source completeness, event/version reconciliation, rights, and quality thresholds remain unresolved. No corpus-wide release or semantic validation is claimed by this change.
